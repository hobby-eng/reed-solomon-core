# Changelog

## 0.1.1 — 2026-11-24

- A product or an inverse in a field made with `Field::binary` takes the same time whatever its elements: the optimiser had turned a mask into a jump on a bit, so that checking or correcting a word in such a field took longer for some symbols than for others. The named fields were not affected.
- A vote whose holders change from block to block, as many as before, keeps its search and its buffers and computes only their weights again. A pile whose damage moves between the shards no longer costs two new protected buffers a block: with `protect`, a vote whose two holders change at every block took 2 to 4 ns per step instead of 115 to 330 in the audit's measurement. The steps and their bound are unchanged.
- The dependencies take compatible requirements instead of exact versions, so that a program can resolve their later patch releases and their fixes. secret-protection-core is at least 0.1.1, whose own dependencies are no longer exact either. `Cargo.lock` holds the versions the tests ran with: secret-protection-core 0.1.1, zeroize 1.9.2 and landlock 0.4.8, and, for the tests only, ahash 0.7.9 and smallvec 1.16.6.
- The release workflow runs the CI gates, and `examples/timing.rs`, which checks that products and inverses take the same time whatever their elements, before it packages and attests a tag.
- Documents: what a vote whose number of holders changes costs, and the limit on mappings; which fields have a shard file; that a marked symbol must hold an element of the field; what the re-exported `Zeroize` is for; how to report a problem; links that stay valid in the package; the README's contents; the notices of cfg-if and of libc through cpufeatures. The package leaves out the instructions for development tools (`AGENTS.md`).

## 0.1.0 — 2026-11-16

The first release. Version 1 of the shard file format is fixed.

- The code: shard `i` is the value at point `i` of the polynomial that the `k` data shards fix. Writing a set, rebuilding from any `k` shards, making a lost shard again and giving a set more shards are one interpolation.
- Three fields: GF(2^8) with 0x11D and GF(2^16) as an extension of it, with the same bytes as `reed-solomon-erasure` 6.0.0, and GF(2^11) with 0x805, whose symbol is one word of a list of 2,048 words.
- Work on the caller's buffers, stripe by stripe, without an allocator; whole-shard helpers with the `alloc` feature.
- A check that shards belong to one set, and the correction of wrong symbols at unknown places (Berlekamp–Massey), with a stated bound on its working memory.
- A scaling kernel that works on eight bytes at a time by shifts and masks and never branches on, or indexes a table by, the bytes of a shard.
- The shard file, version 1: a header with the set's identifier, `k`, the shard's number, the data length and an optional SHA-256 of the data; the shard in blocks with a CRC-32C each, tied to the file and the block's number; the header a second time and a trailer at the end. `Header::file` writes one; `ShardFile::read` reads one that is damaged, cut short or followed by other bytes, by whichever copy of the header holds, and never chooses between two that differ.
- Sets of shard files: `SetFacts::files` writes the files of a set from its data; `Pile::new` sorts any files into sets by the facts they state, whatever their order; `FileSet::rebuild` puts the data together block by block from whichever files hold each block, compares every spare shard and refuses shards that are not one set; `FileSet::repair` makes a lost file again or a new one. Files of one shard fill each other in, and where they differ the shard is left out of that block. `FileSet::stretches` tells which parts of a set still rebuild. A limit in steps, counted by a stated rule, is checked before any work, and every list is asked for fallibly.
- `FileSet::vote` names the files that differ from what the shards of a set share, block by block, wherever spare shards are at hand to tell, and of the files of one shard that differ the ones the other shards do not confirm; `FileSet::without` is the set without the files a program leaves out.
- A shard that is among the sources is copied by `Sources::build`, not summed over all of them.
- The SHA-256 of the data, by the `sha2` crate: `SetFacts::for_data` computes it, `SetFacts::files` and `SetFacts::files_into` write no file for data of another digest, facts that state a digest for data of 2^61 bytes or more are refused, and `FileSet::rebuild` compares it with exactly the data's bytes before it gives the data back. `Rebuilt::digest` says whether it matched or was not stated (`DigestCheck`); data of another digest is `SetError::Digest`.
- Gaps at known places: `Code::plan` takes symbols marked unreadable inside a shard, puts a column right when `2e + s ≤ m − k`, and lists every change (place and whether it was filled or replaced; what was read and what it becomes stay in the list's buffer, shown through `Changes::values` or, on a protected stack, `strict::values`) without changing anything. A plan is bound by a SHA-256 fingerprint to the code and the shards it was made for: `Code::apply` compares it and then checks each change, and writes nothing unless both fit.
- `FileSet::repairs` makes several lost files of a set in one pass over its blocks, counted by `FileSet::repairs_work`.
- Fields of a size the program chooses: `Field::binary(m, polynomial)` is GF(2^m) for `m` from 2 to 16, by the standard primitive polynomial of the degree or one the program gives, checked to be primitive; symbols of one byte up to `m` = 8 and two bytes above, scaled by the same kernel without tables or branches on the bytes.
- Secrets in memory: every result that holds data comes in a `Buffer`, and so does every working buffer of bytes that follow from data, the short lists of both decoders included, cut from one room per search that the `bytemuck` crate shows as numbers; `Sources::agrees_in` compares in a room the caller gives, of `COMPARED` bytes. With the `protect` feature, on by default, a `Buffer` lies in pages of its own from the `secret-protection-core` crate: between guard pages, locked where the system's lock limit allows, and kept out of core dumps and child processes as far as each system allows; without it, in the allocator's memory. The changes of a plan are a `Changes` list that lies in such a buffer. A buffer and a list say whether they are locked, and a buffer whether it is out of core dumps and of child processes. A call that gets no memory, the working memory of `Code::correct` and `Code::plan` included, answers with a `Memory` error. Everything else that held data is wiped when let go (`zeroize`), the SHA-256 state is wiped, no `Debug` text shows data, and single field elements are multiplied and inverted without tables or branches on their values. `reed_solomon_core::protect` passes the protection crate on, for a program that also isolates its process.
- The strict mode, `reed_solomon_core::strict`, for a secret in the open: the calls of both codes, of shard files and of sets, which take and give the secret only in memory the crate can check (`Buffer`, `protect::Secret`, `protect::WorkArea`; a `Vec` does not compile). Every buffer given and every one a call makes must be locked and kept out of child processes and, where the system can, out of dumps, or the call answers `Unprotected` before the first secret byte is written; nothing falls back to other memory. Each call runs on a protected stack of its own, which Linux, Android, macOS and iOS give; on Windows, in WebAssembly and on other systems every strict call on the bytes of a secret refuses. A strict `ShardFile` checks its blocks there too, `FileSet::without` compares files there, and a digest that differs is `SetError::DigestDiffers`, without the digest of the data. Headers and facts a strict call gives stay strict types; `as_portable` is the explicit step to the root types. Where no protected stack is given, making a code and reading public facts still work.
- A self-test of known answers: 17 checks with an allocator, 11 without. It asks for no memory of its own; a refusal of memory to a call it checks fails that check.
- A second construction, `CyclicCode`: a shortened systematic cyclic Reed–Solomon code with a field, a first root and a step between roots, whose check symbols are the remainder of the data divided by its generator, as QR codes and printed repair words compute them. It encodes, checks, corrects wrong symbols at unknown places and plans the changes for marked gaps and for shards lost whole with them, under `2e + s ≤ parity`, with every result checked by its syndromes. A lost shard is named by its place and written anew, whatever it holds. Checking and correcting hold one stripe of syndromes, at most 4 MiB, whatever the length of the shards; a plan lists every change. It gives the published repair words of MHFE-REPAIR-1 and the parity of the QR example of ISO/IEC 18004. `CodeError::Shape` refuses lengths, fields and roots that make no such code. docs/API.md says which construction to choose. `CyclicWords` is the same code without an allocator: the generator in the caller's slice, the check shards into its buffers, the check of words in its room, and one word put right in place.
- `SetFacts::files_into`, `FileSet::rebuild_into` and `FileSet::repairs_into` write the files of a set, its data and lost files into outputs the program gives, such as mappings of files, holding one block at a time, so that sets larger than memory are written, rebuilt and repaired; `Header::file_into` writes one file without an allocator. An output counts only when the call succeeds: a refusal before the work leaves it as it was, one during the work or by the checks after it leaves zeros. `files`, `rebuild`, `repairs` and `file` are these calls into `Buffer`s; `SetError::Output` and `FileError::Output` refuse outputs that are not one for each result of its length, and `Written` reports what a rebuild in place found.
- A test checks by exhaustion that the arithmetic of the named fields and of the standard field of each degree is a field, the kernel that scales shards included, for every pair of elements and every triple of basis elements; the fields above 4,096 elements before a release.
- Fuzz targets under `fuzz/` for shard files, damaged sets, plans, the cyclic code and every public call that takes numbers, lengths or bytes, with the calls that write in place; CI checks and lints them, lints the crate for a target without an operating system, and holds both lock files against RustSec.
- `examples/measure.rs` measures the main calls; docs/TECHNICAL.md has their times and memory on one machine, natively and in WebAssembly, and what a step of the work limit takes.
